Violent crime rates

Start with the broader pattern. Each block group is classified by its violent crime rate relative to block groups elsewhere in the same city.

Describe before explaining: identify the general level, the spatial pattern, and where higher or lower values occur. Then compare the two neighborhoods and the areas immediately around them.

What the categories mean: violent crime includes homicide, aggravated assault, and robbery. Rates are per 1,000 residents and averaged across 2021–2023. The five categories represent relative positions within each city: Very Low, Low, Moderate, High, and Very High.

Keep the inference descriptive: these maps can support, complicate, or contradict your earlier predictions, but they do not show that any neighborhood characteristic caused crime.

Incident patterns

Focus on spatial distribution: look for clustering, dispersion, corridors, isolated pockets, and differences in where incidents concentrate. The number of points matters, but do not let volume substitute for describing the pattern.

Return to your Opportunity analysis: compare the incident pattern with the micro-places, generators or attractors, nodes, paths, and edges you identified earlier. If relevant, also consider whether incidents appear to align with sharp or fuzzy racial-ethnic boundaries from the Segregation module.

Map controls: use the Street map / Satellite toggle when built-environment context helps you interpret a cluster. Your Word document will capture the map in whichever view you leave selected.
