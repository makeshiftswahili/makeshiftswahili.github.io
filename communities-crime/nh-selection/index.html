Neighborhood Selection

Select two neighborhoods from the same city for your semester-long comparative analysis. Use the map and basemap controls to inspect each neighborhood before making your final choice.

Neighborhood Choice Requirements

  1. Both neighborhoods must be located within the same city. They may be in the same general sub-region of the city (e.g., “Eastside”), but they must not be contiguous.
  2. Neighborhoods must be of a reasonable spatial scale: avoid choosing neighborhoods that are unusually small relative to other neighborhoods in the city. Avoid choosing one geographically large neighborhood and one geographically small neighborhood—they can be different sizes but should be roughly comparable.
  3. Neighborhoods may include mixed land uses (e.g., residential, commercial/shopping district, industrial, schools), but they must include a substantial residential presence. Industrial, commercial, or natural areas with very sparse residential populations are not permitted. A good rule of thumb is - if it doesn't align with what comes to mind when you think of "neighborhood," it's probably not an appropriate choice. However, having at least one neighborhood with some non-residential land use will be useful for your analysis.

Tip: Since this is a comparative analysis, avoid choosing neighborhoods you know to be similar in terms of sociodemographic composition, reputation, land use, etc. Choosing neighborhoods from two different regions of the city often provides a good contrast. Differences are interesting, give you more to talk about, and end up making the analysis easier.
